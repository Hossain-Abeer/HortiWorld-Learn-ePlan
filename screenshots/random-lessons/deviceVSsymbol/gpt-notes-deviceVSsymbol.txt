1. THE SHORT ANSWER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- SYMBOLS are the pure graphics (blue line art) drawn on the page. They 
  represent the electrical "function" (e.g., "a circuit breaker goes here").
- DEVICES are the commercial, real-world components (e.g., a specific 
  Siemens 3-pole 16A breaker). 

A Device is built out of one or more Symbols and links them to a specific 
Part Number in the database.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHAT EACH ONE DOES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SYMBOLS (The Blue Line Art)
- Found in the Symbol Library (IEC_symbol).
- Has no manufacturer, no price, and no physical dimensions.
- It only has connection points (pins) and a graphic.
- If you insert a raw symbol, you just get a drawing with a device tag (like 
  -Q1). You cannot generate a Bill of Materials (BOM) from it.

DEVICES (The Real Components)
- Found in the Devices/Insert Center (often linked to the EPLAN Data Portal).
- Has a real Part Number (e.g., SIE.5SL4320-7RC).
- Carries commercial data: manufacturer, price, weight, and technical specs.
- Carries physical data: terminal layout, dimensions (crucial for cabinet 
  layout / Pro Panel).
- When you insert a Device, EPLAN places the correct graphical Symbol AND 
  attaches the commercial Part to it simultaneously.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. MENTAL MODEL (Compared to KiCAD / PCB Design)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- EPLAN SYMBOL = KiCAD Schematic Symbol (`.kicad_sym`). Just the logical 
  drawing.
- EPLAN DEVICE = KiCAD Component + Footprint Assignment. It ties the 
  schematic drawing to a physical part in the BOM.

The difference is that EPLAN centralizes this. In KiCAD, you place a symbol 
and then use CvPcb to assign a footprint. In EPLAN, the "Device" master data 
already contains both the schematic symbol AND the physical data.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHEN TO USE WHICH?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
USE SYMBOLS when:
- You are doing a quick sketch or a rough draft and don't care about the 
  manufacturer yet.
- You need a custom graphical shape that doesn't exist in the standard 
  device database.

USE DEVICES when:
- You are doing actual engineering for a real panel (like at Hortiworld).
- You need to generate a BOM (Bill of Materials) or terminal diagrams.
- You need to eventually route wires in a 3D cabinet (Pro Panel).
- You want the software to auto-populate the technical specs (voltage, 
  current rating) into your project.

RULE OF THUMB: 
Always use Devices for production projects. Use Symbols only for quick 
placeholders or custom graphics.
