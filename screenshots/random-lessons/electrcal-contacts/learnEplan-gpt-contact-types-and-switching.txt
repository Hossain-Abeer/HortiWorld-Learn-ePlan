1. THE TYPES OF CONTACTS EXPLAINED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- COIL: The actuator. It's an electromagnet. When you apply voltage to the 
  coil (usually A1/A2), it creates a magnetic field that physically pulls 
  the contacts closed or open.
- NO CONTACT (Normally Open): A switch that is OPEN by default. It only 
  allows current to flow when the coil is energized or the button is pushed. 
  (Used for "Start" buttons or turning loads on).
- NC CONTACT (Normally Closed): A switch that is CLOSED by default. It 
  allows current to flow until it is actuated, at which point it OPENS and 
  breaks the circuit. (Used for "Stop" buttons, E-stops, or overloads).
- CHANGE-OVER CONTACT: A combination of NO and NC. It has 3 connections 
  (Common, NO, NC). When actuated, it switches the common connection from 
  one path to the other (like a 3-way switch or SPDT).

3. WHY PUT A NO CONTACT AFTER A CIRCUIT BREAKER?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Looking at Image 3: 
Incoming Power (R, Y, B) -> MCB (Breaker) -> -Q2 (3-Pole NO Contact) -> Load

You asked: *If there's a circuit breaker already, what's the function of the 
NO contact?*

They serve two completely different purposes:
1. PROTECTION (The MCB): A Miniature Circuit Breaker is a safety device. It 
   is designed to trip ONLY during an overload or a short circuit. You do 
   not (and should not) use an MCB to turn a motor on and off every day. They 
   wear out quickly if used as switches.
2. CONTROL (The NO Contact): The NO contact (likely a Contactor, though here 
   tagged `-Q2`) is the operational switch. It is controlled by the PLC or 
   a pushbutton. It turns the power to the load ON and OFF during normal 
   operation. 

SEQUENCE OF POWER:
The power flows through the MCB first for safety. Then it goes to the NO 
contact, which acts as the "valve" to turn the motor on or off. You want 
the protection *before* the control.

(Also, notice how Phase N and PE do NOT go through the NO contact in Image 3. 
In a standard 3-phase motor, you only switch the 3 live phases. N is usually 
not needed for the motor itself, and PE must always be permanently connected 
for safety, never switched).
