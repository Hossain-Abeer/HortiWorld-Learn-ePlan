1. YOUR CATCH ON "STAR" VS "START"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You are absolutely correct. The tutorial almost certainly had a typo or a 
misleading label by writing "START" for the NC contact. 
You named it "STAR" (indicating the NC contact of the Star contactor). This is 
the correct engineering practice. The NC contact of the Star contactor is used 
to interlock the Delta contactor, ensuring they never close at the same time.

2. HOW K1 APPEARS IN BOTH PAGES & INTERLINKING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In EPLAN, a "Device" is a logical concept, not a graphical one. 
- K1 is a single device (a contactor).
- On Page 4 (Power circuit), K1 is represented by its main power contacts 
  (poles 1-2, 3-4, 5-6).
- On Page 5 (Control circuit), K1 is represented by its coil (A1-A2) and its 
  auxiliary NO contact (13-14).
Because you gave them all the exact same Device Tag (-K1), EPLAN links them. 
They are physically different drawings, but logically the exact same component.
The Device Overview (the list of pins at the bottom right of Page 5) shows 
this: pins 1-2, 3-4, 5-6 are marked /4.1 (Page 4, column 1) and pin 13-14 is 
marked /5.3 (Page 5, column 3).

3. THE CONTROL CIRCUIT NETWORK (HOW IT OPERATES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Look at the circuit on Page 5:
- Power flows from R through OLR (Overload NC contact) and STOP (NC button).
- Pressing START (NO) energizes the K1 coil.
- K1's NO contact (13-14) bypasses the START button (the "seal-in" circuit). 
  The coil stays energized even after you release the button.
- Below the seal-in, the circuit splits into two parallel paths:
  Path A (Star): Power flows through the NC contact of DELTA (labeled "DELTA" 
  in your drawing) to energize the STAR coil.
  Path B (Delta): Power flows through the NC contact of STAR (the one you 
  corrected) to energize the DELTA coil.

4. THE INTERLOCKING MECHANISM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Notice the NC contacts labeled "STAR" and "DELTA" in the control circuit. 
These are electrical interlocks.
- If the STAR coil is energized, its NC contact opens. This cuts power to the 
  DELTA coil. Delta cannot turn on while Star is running.
- If the DELTA coil is energized, its NC contact opens. This cuts power to the 
  STAR coil.
This guarantees that even if the PLC makes a software error and tries to turn 
on both, the hardware itself will prevent it (dead short prevention).

5. THE STARTUP SEQUENCE (What the PLC does)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. PLC sends a signal to START. K1 coil energizes. Main contactor K1 closes.
2. Because DELTA is off, its NC contact is closed. Power flows to the STAR coil.
3. Motor starts in Star (low current).
4. A timer in the PLC waits 5 seconds.
5. PLC breaks the signal to the STAR coil. Star contactor opens.
6. STAR's NC contact closes.
7. Power now flows to the DELTA coil. Delta contactor closes.
8. Motor runs in Delta (full power).
