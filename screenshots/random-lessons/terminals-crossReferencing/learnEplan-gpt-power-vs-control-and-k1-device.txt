1. THE JOURNEY OF CURRENT: POWER VS. CONTROL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You are confusing the Main Power Circuit (Page 4) with the Control Circuit 
(Page 5). They are two separate electrical loops.

- Page 4 (Power): This is the heavy-duty 400V loop. R, Y, B go through the 
  heavy metal contacts of K1, through the heating elements of the OLR, and 
  into the motor.
- Page 5 (Control): This is the low-power loop (usually 230V or 24V). It is 
  tapped off one phase (R) and Neutral (N). It does NOT go to the motor. It 
  goes only to the coils, buttons, and sensors.

When I said "Power flows from R through OLR...", I meant *control power*. 
The OLR symbol on Page 5 is not the main heating element; it is the small 
auxiliary NC switch built into the side of the OLR. If the main OLR heats up 
and trips, this little switch opens, killing the control circuit and dropping 
K1.

2. TRACING THE CONTROL CIRCUIT (PAGE 5)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Let's trace the exact path from R to N on Page 5:

1. Current enters from R.
2. Passes through the OLR auxiliary NC contact. (Closed during normal running).
3. Passes through the STOP button (NC). (Closed unless pressed).
4. Arrives at a T-junction. The current is now waiting.
5. Pressing the START button (NO) allows current to flow to the K1 coil.
6. The K1 coil energizes (A1 to A2), connecting to N, completing the circuit.
7. Because K1 is energized, its main contacts on Page 4 close (motor spins).
8. Because K1 is energized, its 13-14 NO contact on Page 5 closes.
9. You release the START button. The current now bypasses the START button 
   and flows through the K1 13-14 contact to keep the coil energized. This 
   is called a "Seal-in" or "Self-holding" circuit.

3. HOW CAN "K1" BE A 3-PHASE SWITCH, A COIL, AND A 1-PHASE SWITCH?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
No, "K1" is not a voltage node. 
"K1" is a PHYSICAL DEVICE. It is a single, physical block of plastic and 
copper sitting in the control cabinet (a Contactor).

Imagine holding a real contactor in your hand. It has:
- A big 3-phase block on the bottom for the motor (drawn on Page 4).
- A small coil on top to turn it on (drawn on Page 5).
- Small auxiliary switches on the front (drawn on Page 5).

In EPLAN, you are drawing the *wiring* of this physical device. 
Because the software needs to know that the 3-phase switch on Page 4, the 
coil on Page 5, and the 1-phase switch on Page 5 are all physically the same 
piece of plastic, you give them all the EXACT SAME name: -K1.

If you gave them different names, EPLAN would think you have three separate 
devices in the cabinet. By sharing the name -K1, EPLAN links them together. 
When the coil gets power on Page 5, EPLAN knows the main contacts on Page 4 
close.

4. THE INTERLOCKING MECHANISM (PUTTING IT TOGETHER)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Look at the branches below the START button:
- In series with the STAR coil, there is an NC contact of DELTA.
- In series with the DELTA coil, there is an NC contact of STAR.

When the STAR coil is energized, the STAR NC contact opens. This physically 
cuts the wire before the DELTA coil can get power. This is how the hardware 
guarantees they never turn on at the same time, preventing a dead short 
circuit on Page 4.
