1. THE DIFFERENCE IN THE PROPERTIES PANEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Your observation is correct. You used two different types of symbols, which 
is why the properties panel looks different.

- Page 2 and Page 4 (Terminals): You used a "Terminal" symbol from the 
  library. You can see the Full DT is =CA1+EAA-R-R1. This means it represents 
  a physical terminal block (e.g., Terminal 1 on the R phase). Because it is a 
  physical device, EPLAN categorizes it as a "Terminal" with connection 
  points.
- Page 5 (Interruption Point): You used an "Interruption point" symbol. The 
  Full DT is =CA1+EAA-R. It is a purely logical symbol. It tells EPLAN "this 
  wire continues to another page" without representing a physical terminal 
  block. 

In the tutorial, the creator likely used terminals on Page 2 and 4 because 
power physically enters and exits the cabinet through terminal blocks. On 
Page 5, they just wanted to show the potential continuing to another sheet, 
so an interruption point is sufficient.

2. THE TUTORIAL'S ACTION: CROSS-REFERENCE FUNCTION > FORWARD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When the tutorial right-clicked the end point of the terminal on Page 4 and 
chose "Cross-reference function > forward", they were manually telling EPLAN:
"This terminal is the start of a wire, point the arrow forward to show 
where it goes next."

By default, EPLAN might just draw a terminal and leave the wire hanging. 
Using the "Cross-reference function" tool forces EPLAN to look for another 
terminal or interruption point with the exact same potential name (R) and 
generate the jump-link (e.g., R/2.9). "Forward" dictates the direction of 
the arrow and that this terminal is the "source" feeding the next page.

3. COULD YOU DO IT FROM PAGE 2 INSTEAD OF CREATING A NEW ONE ON PAGE 4?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Yes. Functionally, copying and pasting the terminal from Page 2 to Page 4 is 
identical to creating a new one, as long as the Potential Name (R) and 
Terminal Designation (R1) match.

The reason the tutorial created a new one on Page 4 is purely for the sake of 
demonstration. In real engineering, you would likely copy-paste the whole 
power supply block from Page 2 to Page 4 to maintain visual consistency. As 
long as they share the same Full DT (=CA1+EAA-R-R1), EPLAN treats them as two 
representations of the exact same physical terminal.

4. INTERPRETING THE CROSS-REFERENCE LIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Look at the table in your fourth screenshot. It shows three entries for the 
name =CA1+EAA-R:
- Placed at =CA1+EAA/2.9 (Page 2, Row 9)
- Placed at =CA1+EAA/4.9 (Page 4, Row 9)
- Placed at =CA1+EAA/5.0 (Page 5, Row 0)

What this means:
EPLAN has successfully recognized that the potential "R" exists on all three 
pages. It has grouped them together. 

Notice that the Name column says =CA1+EAA-R, NOT =CA1+EAA-R-R1. This means 
EPLAN is tracking the POTENTIAL (the wire itself), not just the physical 
terminal. Because Page 5 used an interruption point instead of a terminal, 
EPLAN included it in the potential's cross-reference list, proving that the 
logical link between the physical terminals (Pages 2 and 4) and the logical 
interruption (Page 5) is successful. 

You have successfully linked the power across the sheets.
