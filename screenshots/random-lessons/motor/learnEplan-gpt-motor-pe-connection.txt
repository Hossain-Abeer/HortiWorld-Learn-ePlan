1. THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THE TWO IMAGES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Electrically: There is ZERO difference.
Visually: The difference is purely which pin connection point the PE wire is 
attached to on the motor symbol.
- Image 1: The PE wire is connected to a pin on the *right side* of the 
  motor circle.
- Image 2: The PE wire is connected to a pin at the *bottom* of the motor 
  circle.

EPLAN uses different symbol variants for the same physical device. Image 2 
(PE at the bottom) is the standard IEC convention. It looks cleaner and 
prevents the wire from crossing the 3-phase lines. The YouTuber likely 
swapped the symbol variant or moved the connection point in the symbol 
properties.

(Note: The blue wire coming in from the top left is labeled "PB" in the 
tutorial. This is almost certainly a typo by the creator. It should be "PE". 
Standard IEC colors: R=Black/Grey, Y=Black/Grey, B=Black/Grey, N=Blue, 
PE=Green/Yellow).

2. WHY IS THE EARTH WIRE CONNECTED?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Safety. The PE (Protective Earth) wire is connected to the metal chassis 
(or stator housing) of the motor. 

If the internal insulation of the motor fails, a live phase wire (R, Y, or 
B) could touch the metal casing. Without PE, the casing becomes "live" 
(400V/230V). If a worker touches it, the current flows through their body 
to the ground, causing severe shock or death.

With PE connected, the fault current flows straight to earth. This creates 
a massive current spike (a short circuit) that instantly trips the MCB 
(Circuit Breaker) or blows the fuse, shutting off power safely before 
anyone gets hurt.

3. WHAT WOULD HAPPEN IF THE OTHER WAS CONNECTED?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
There are two interpretations of your question:

A) What if you swapped the pin connections (e.g., connected Phase R to 
   the PE pin, and PE to the Phase pin)?
   Result: The moment you turn on the power, Phase R goes directly to Earth. 
   This is a dead short circuit to ground. The MCB will trip instantly (or 
   blow up if it's a cheap breaker).

B) What if you used the symbol from Image 1 instead of Image 2?
   Result: Electrically nothing changes. The motor still operates, and the 
   casing is still grounded. However, visually, the wire might overlap with 
   other lines or look messy. It is purely an aesthetic choice in EPLAN.

RULE OF THUMB: 
Always connect PE to the motor casing. In standard European schematics, 
PE is drawn at the bottom. Also, PE must NEVER be switched by a contactor 
or breaker. It goes straight from the terminal block to the motor.
