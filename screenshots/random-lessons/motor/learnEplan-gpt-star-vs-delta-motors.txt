1. THE BIG MISCONCEPTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
There is no such thing as a "Star motor" or a "Delta motor". A standard 
3-phase AC induction motor is physically identical regardless of how it is 
wired. 

When engineers say "Star" or "Delta," they are referring to **how the 6 
wires in the motor's terminal box are connected to each other and to the 
power supply.** The motor itself has 3 separate coils (windings). 

2. THE TERMINAL BOX (The 6 Wires)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A motor that can do both has 6 terminals in its junction box:
- U1, V1, W1 (the beginnings of the 3 coils)
- U2, V2, W2 (the ends of the 3 coils)

Depending on how you place the metal shorting links in the terminal box, you 
create either a Star or Delta connection.

3. THE THREE CONFIGURATIONS EXPLAINED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A) DELTA (Δ) - The Standard Running Mode
   - How it's wired: U2 connects to V1, V2 connects to W1, W2 connects to U1. 
     (Drawing a triangle).
   - Voltage across coil: Full line voltage (e.g., 400V).
   - Result: The motor draws full current and produces **full torque**. 
   - Use case: This is how the motor runs 90% of the time during normal 
     operation.

B) STAR (Y) - The Starting Mode (or High-Voltage Mode)
   - How it's wired: U2, V2, and W2 are all tied together in the center. 
     U1, V1, W1 connect to the three power phases. (Drawing a Y).
   - Voltage across coil: Reduced voltage (Line voltage / √3). So, 400V 
     becomes 230V per coil.
   - Result: The motor draws only 1/3 of the current and produces only 1/3 
     of the torque.
   - Use case: Used briefly during startup to prevent a massive power spike, 
     OR used permanently if the motor is rated for a higher voltage (e.g., a 
     400V/690V motor wired in Star on a 690V grid).

C) STAR-DELTA (Y-Δ) - The Starting System
   - This isn't a motor type; it's a *system* (the schematic you just drew). 
   - You use a special motor with 6 wires brought out to the panel.
   - You use contactors to physically change the wiring from Star to Delta 
     while the motor is running.
   - Starts in Star (low current), runs in Delta (full power).

4. HOW TO READ A MOTOR NAMEPLATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Look at the voltage rating on a motor. It usually looks like this:
- **230V Δ / 400V Y** (Older European standard)
- **400V Δ / 690V Y** (Modern European standard)

What this means:
- If your grid is 400V, and you use a Star-Delta starter, you wire it in 
  Delta for normal running. During startup, you wire it in Star, which drops 
  the voltage across the coils from 400V down to 230V (safe for starting).
- If you only have a Direct-On-Line (DOL) starter, you just use metal links 
  to wire it permanently in Delta. 

5. SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Same physical motor.
- Delta = Full power, high startup current (Running mode).
- Star = Low power, low startup current (Starting mode).
- Star-Delta = Using a panel with contactors to automatically switch from 
  Star to Delta as the motor spins up.
