1. THE BREAKDOWN OF MOTOR TYPES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The symbols in the library are categorized by **Connection Points (CP)** 
and whether they explicitly draw the **Protective Earth (PE)** and the 
internal **Windings**.

- **Motor without PE and winding, 2 connection points:**
  Represents a highly simplified DC motor (+, -) or a single-phase AC motor. 
  It has no ground drawn and shows just a plain circle. Rarely used in 
  modern industrial panels.

- **Motor with PE, 3 connection points:**
  Typically represents a single-phase AC motor (Live, Neutral, Earth). 
  Alternatively, some libraries use this for a simplified 3-phase motor 
  where the 3 phases connect, and the PE is visually implied or placed 
  separately.

- **Motor with PE, 4 connection points:**
  **This is the standard 3-phase AC motor.** It has connections for U, V, 
  W (the three phases) and a 4th connection at the bottom of the circle for 
  PE. This is the one you will use 90% of the time at Hortiworld.

- **Motor without PE, 4 connection points:**
  A 3-phase motor, but the 4th connection is not Earth. It could represent 
  a motor with a built-in thermal sensor (PTC), an electromagnetic brake, 
  or a 4-wire configuration. PE must be connected externally.

- **Motor, variable:**
  A generic placeholder. You can double-click it and change the number of 
  connection points to whatever you need. Useful for complex motors (like 
  a 6-wire motor for Star-Delta starting, where you need U1,V1,W1 and 
  U2,V2,W2).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE ROLE OF "WINDING" IN THE SYMBOL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Some symbols show zig-zag lines or loops inside the circle. This represents 
the actual physical copper windings of the motor. 
- A plain circle (without winding) is the most common IEC standard for a 
  generalized motor.
- A circle with windings is used in specific textbooks or older standards 
  to emphasize the internal electrical structure.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHICH ONE TO USE? (Rule of Thumb)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- For a standard 3-phase conveyor motor or pump motor: Use **Motor with 
  PE, 4 connection points**. 
- For a Star-Delta starter (where 6 wires go to the motor): Use **Motor, 
  variable** and set it to 6 connection points (or search for a dedicated 
  Star-Delta motor symbol).
- Remember: The motor symbol's connection numbers (1, 2, 3, 4) don't 
  inherently mean U, V, W. You map the phase wires to the pins inside the 
  symbol's properties.
