1. YOUR MENTAL MODEL IS ALMOST CORRECT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You are 100% right: no human manually flips these switches. In industrial 
panels, a PLC (or a timer relay) controls them using binary 24V DC signals. 
The operator just presses a "Start" button.

The confusion comes from thinking of K1, Star, and Delta as separate 
"on/off" switches. They are not. They are **routing switches**. 

Here are their exact roles:
- **K1 (Main Contactor):** The Master Power Switch. If K1 is open, the motor 
  is completely dead, regardless of what Star and Delta are doing.
- **Star Contactor (K3):** The "Starting" router. It connects the ends of 
  the motor coils together.
- **Delta Contactor (K2):** The "Running" router. It connects the ends of 
  the motor coils back to the main power.

2. THE SEQUENCE OF OPERATION (WHAT THE PLC DOES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Here is the exact logic sequence that happens inside the PLC when an 
operator presses "START":

Step 1: PLC turns ON the Star contactor. (The motor coils are now configured 
        in Star).
Step 2: PLC turns ON the Main contactor (K1). 
        *Result: The motor starts turning gently with low current.*
Step 3: PLC starts a timer (e.g., 5 seconds). The motor accelerates.
Step 4: Timer finishes. PLC turns OFF the Star contactor.
Step 5: PLC turns ON the Delta contactor.
        *Result: The motor switches to full power and runs normally.*

3. WHY DO WE NEED K1 IF WE HAVE STAR AND DELTA?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Look at your schematic. The power comes in from the top (R, Y, B) and goes 
through K1 first. 

- The Star and Delta contactors are placed *downstream* of K1. They only 
  route the power between the motor's 6 terminals. 
- **K1 is the safety gate.** If the OLR (overload relay) detects a jammed 
  motor, it sends a signal to the PLC, which instantly drops K1. This kills 
  power to the entire motor.
- Furthermore, K1 provides a physical break between the 400V grid and the 
  motor. If you only used Star and Delta to turn the motor on and off, you 
  would be switching high voltage directly at the motor windings, which is 
  bad practice and creates massive electrical arcing.

4. THE CATASTROPHE PREVENTED BY THE PLC (INTERLOCKING)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
If the PLC turns on Star and Delta at the exact same time, it connects R, Y, 
and B directly together. This is a **dead short circuit** (400V phase-to-
phase). It will explode the contactors.

To prevent this, the PLC code has a "Dead Time" (usually 50 to 100 
milliseconds) between turning off Star and turning on Delta. 
Additionally, in the real world, panel builders install a physical 
**Mechanical Interlock** (a plastic lever) between the Star and Delta 
contactors so they physically cannot close at the same time, even if the 
PLC makes a mistake.
