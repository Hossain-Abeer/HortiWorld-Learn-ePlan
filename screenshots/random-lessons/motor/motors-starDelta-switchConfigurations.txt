1. WHY DO WE NEED A STAR-DELTA STARTER?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When you turn on a large 3-phase motor directly (Direct-On-Line / DOL), it 
draws an "inrush current" that is 6 to 8 times its normal running current. 
For a 50A motor, that's a 300A spike. This causes massive voltage dips in the 
greenhouse grid and puts immense mechanical stress on the motor and gearbox.

The Star-Delta starter is a classic, cost-effective way to reduce that 
startup current to about 1/3 of the DOL spike. 

2. THE CONCEPT: HOW THE MOTOR WINDINGS WORK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A Star-Delta motor is physically built with 6 wires coming out of it, 
representing 3 separate coils (windings):
- Coil 1: U1 - U2
- Coil 2: V1 - V2
- Coil 3: W1 - W2

By changing how we connect these 6 wires, we change the voltage across each 
individual coil:
- STAR (Y) Configuration: We tie the ends (U2, V2, W2) together into a single 
  "neutral" point. The voltage across each coil is 230V (400V / √3). 
  *Result: Low current, low torque. Good for starting.*
- DELTA (Δ) Configuration: We connect the ends to the next phase (U2 to V1, 
  V2 to W1, W2 to U1). The voltage across each coil is the full 400V. 
  *Result: High current, full torque. Good for running.*

3. WHY ARE THERE TWO CONTACTORS? (STAR & DELTA)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You need both because the motor must *start* in Star, and then *run* in Delta.
- **Star Contactor (usually K3):** Its job is to short U2, V2, and W2 
  together. You will notice in your schematic it connects all three bottom 
  pins to a single common line. It is closed ONLY during the startup phase.
- **Delta Contactor (usually K2):** Its job is to connect U2, V2, and W2 to 
  the phases (L1, L2, L3) to form the triangle. It is closed ONLY when the 
  motor is up to speed and running normally.

4. WHAT IF YOU USED ONLY ONE? (WHY NOT JUST ONE?)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- If you ONLY used the Star contactor: The motor would only ever see 230V 
  per coil. It would start gently, but it would produce only 1/3 of its 
  rated torque. The moment you put a load on it, it would stall. (Never used 
  for running).
- If you ONLY used the Delta contactor: You are back to a Direct-On-Line 
  start. You get full torque, but you get the massive, grid-tripping startup 
  current spike. (Never used for starting large motors).

5. THE SEQUENCE OF OPERATION (CRITICAL SAFETY)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
If Star and Delta are closed at the SAME TIME, you create a dead short 
circuit between L1, L2, and L3. The panel will explode. 
Therefore, the control logic (PLC or timer relays) must follow this strict 
sequence:
1. Close Main Contactor (K1) + Star Contactor (K3). (Motor starts in Y).
2. Wait 3-10 seconds (motor accelerates).
3. OPEN Star Contactor (K3). (The motor coasts for a brief moment).
4. CLOSE Delta Contactor (K2). (Motor transitions to full power).

Note: Look at your schematic. The Star and Delta contactors are physically 
wired in a way that one connects the motor terminals together, and the other 
connects them to the power lines. They are mechanically and electrically 
interlocked in the real world so they can never close simultaneously.
